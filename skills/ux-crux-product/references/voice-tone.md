# Product: voice and tone

Platform-agnostic. Covers rules VT01-VT06 from the source catalog — how copy *sounds*, evaluated against the user's journey stage and emotional state, in addition to `core.md`'s content-clarity rules (C01-C06) covering *what* copy says and `web.md`'s C07-C12 covering text-scanning/typography for web-rendered content. See `../shared/evidence-model.md` and `severity-model.md` for how to report findings from these rules.

## Voice & tone

| ID | Rule | Evidence | Default severity | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VT01 | Copy is evaluated in priority order: clear first, concise second, human third. Clever, warm, or stylistically distinctive phrasing SHALL NOT be recommended, or preferred over a plainer alternative, at the expense of clarity about what will happen if the user proceeds. | Strong | Major | Metts & Welfle, *Writing Is Designing* (Rosenfeld Media, 2020), synthesizing product-voice practice at Slack, Adobe, and Facebook |
| VT02 | Tone is evaluated as a contextual, multi-dimensional spectrum (for example: motivational, useful, instructive, reassuring, supportive) driven by the user's journey stage and emotional state, not as one fixed personality applied uniformly across every screen. A high-stress or error-recovery moment calls for a more restrained, instructive, or supportive tone than an onboarding or celebration moment. | Contextual | Major | Metts & Welfle, *Writing Is Designing*; cross-reference `usability/references/core.md` E02-E03 (error-copy tone) and `psychology/references/emotion.md` PE02 (Peak-End, high-intensity moments) rather than duplicating their findings |
| VT03 | Tone is treated as a measurable usability factor, not an unfalsifiable style preference: a factor-analysis study of 862 participants across 76 usability items found "customer service behavior" (politeness, friendliness, respectful tone) to be one of the top four drivers of overall perceived technology usability. A tone finding SHOULD be defended on this basis rather than dismissed as subjective when a stakeholder pushes back. | Research finding | Moderate | Melanie Polkosky, doctoral research on social-cognitive dimensions of usability (as synthesized in Metts & Welfle, *Writing Is Designing*) |
| VT04 | Celebratory, congratulatory, or lifecycle copy (for example a milestone screen or a "year in review" summary) is flagged if it relies on a culturally specific idiom, sitcom or pop-culture reference, or rhetorical humor that is unlikely to translate cleanly or could read as literal/confusing to a non-native or international audience. | Strong | Moderate | Apple HIG Inclusion; Android Localize your app (the same source family cited for `core.md` C05) |
| VT05 | A finding about copy's warmth, personality, or "voice" distinguishes the product's own task-level voice from a separate marketing/brand voice. Appropriately restrained, low-key product copy SHALL NOT be flagged as a tone deficiency for lacking marketing-style personality, provided it remains clear and appropriately human. | Applicability guardrail | N/A (bounds applicability of VT01-VT04) | Metts & Welfle, *Writing Is Designing* (Brand Voice vs. Product Voice distinction) |
| VT06 | A non-error, human/administrative notification carrying inherently unwelcome news (an overdue-payment notice, a policy-violation warning, a scheduled-downtime alert, or a comparable administrative message) may use light, self-aware, disarming tone to soften the message; the absence of such softening is an opportunity, not a defect. Distinct from `usability`'s E02/E03, which require system error copy to identify the actual problem in user language and forbid cute language that obscures the fix — those requirements are unchanged for genuine system errors. This permission never extends to a security-breach or safety-critical notification — light tone there is a Blocker-severity finding, not a style choice. | Contextual | Moderate | Debt-collection-letter tone-softening example (via Sean Adams, *How Design Makes Us Think and Feel and Do Things*) |

**Applicability note:** VT02 is descriptive scaffolding for judging fit, not a license to invent a tone taxonomy per review — when the reviewed evidence does not indicate the user's journey stage or emotional state (for example a bare component library with no surrounding flow context), report a tone finding as `NOT ASSESSABLE` rather than assuming a default tone is wrong.

**Applicability note:** VT05 governs every other rule in this file. Before flagging VT01-VT04, confirm the copy under review is task-level product copy, not brand/marketing copy (a landing page, an ad, a company blog post) — brand voice is out of scope for this skill.

## How the product skill should apply this file

Check VT01 first: does the copy state the outcome clearly, regardless of how the message is styled? Only once clarity is established, check VT02 (does the tone fit this specific moment in the user's journey) and VT04 (does a celebratory or lifecycle message risk a translation/cultural-literalism failure). Use VT03 to support a tone finding with a named empirical source when a stakeholder treats tone as unfalsifiable opinion. Apply VT05 as a standing guardrail: a plain, restrained tone on ordinary task-level copy is not itself a defect. When two lenses would both flag the same copy from different angles (for example this file's VT02 and `usability`'s E02/E03 on the same error message), report it once, citing both, per `review/references/review-model.md`'s cross-lens synthesis guidance. Check VT06 only for non-error, human/administrative bad-news copy (overdue notices, policy-violation warnings, downtime alerts) — never for genuine system error copy, which stays governed by `usability`'s E02/E03 regardless of how unwelcome the news is. Confirm first that the notification is not security-breach or safety-critical before treating light tone as acceptable; on that class of message, light tone is itself the defect VT06 flags.
