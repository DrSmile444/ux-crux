## Why

Donald A. Norman's *Living with Complexity* (MIT Press, 2010) was compared item-by-item (34 extracted knowledge items across 9 chapters) against this project's full existing rule catalog before drafting this proposal. Two prior comparison passes already absorbed a large share of Norman's vocabulary — *Design for a Better World* (affordances-adjacent semi-automation, defaults, error-as-design-failure, aggregate-number fallacies) and *The UX Book* (the Four Kinds of Affordances framework, itself Norman-derived) — so the overlap here is real and is called out explicitly rather than re-added. The comparison surfaced seven genuinely new, checkable rules with no existing home, plus seven citation-only enrichments that sharpen already-covered rules with a stronger or more concrete named source, consistent with this project's established comparison-pass pattern (see `CHANGELOG.md` 0.1.6-0.1.19).

## What Changes

- `usability`: add `AF05` to `references/affordances.md` — a persistent instructional label, warning sticker, or explanatory tooltip added specifically to compensate for a control's own confusing operation is itself evidence the underlying design failed, not a mitigation to credit; legally mandated warnings are excluded. This is the design-side counterpart to the file's existing `AF02` (a *user*-created workaround as a missing-affordance signal) — `AF05` is the same diagnostic logic applied to a *designer*-created band-aid.
- `psychology`: add `PC09` to `references/cognitive.md` — the cumulative cognitive burden of many individually-simple, idiosyncratic interactions across a product (per-item confirmations, inconsistent per-toggle rules, repeated micro-permissions, unrelated password/expiry policies) is evaluated as a system-level load in its own right, not dismissed because any single instance looks trivial in isolation. Distinct from `PC01` (extraneous load within one task), which cannot surface a defect that only exists when many separately-fine instances are multiplied across the whole product.
- `product`: add `IA10` to `references/information-architecture.md` — when a user's real goal spans multiple settings, modules, or subsystems (a multi-device dashboard, a settings area covering several backend systems), controls are grouped and exposed by the user's named activity (e.g. "Watch a movie"), not by which internal module or device owns them; forcing the user to manually orchestrate each subsystem separately to reach one outcome is a design defect, not a training gap. Distinct from `IA01` (navigation/content categorized by user mental models, not internal department structure) — `IA10` applies the same organizing principle specifically to control/settings surfaces for a cross-module activity, which `IA01`'s content-categorization scope does not cover.
- `usability`: add `S13` to `references/core.md`'s System status table — a wait/queue experience (checkout processing, matchmaking, a support-ticket queue, an onboarding delay) sets an explicit, appropriately conservative duration/position expectation, keeps the user meaningfully occupied rather than facing a static/empty state, treats all waiting users under one visibly fair order with no undisclosed line-jumping or paid priority cuts, and ends on a clear, positive resolution. Distinct from `S03`/`S04` (loading-state visual treatment: whether a progress indicator exists and what type) and `S06` (state completeness): `S13` addresses the psychological wait-experience principles governing any queue or hold, which a technically-correct progress indicator can still fail (for example, an accurate spinner with no fairness guarantee or no occupied-time content).
- `usability`: add `N23R` to `references/core.md`'s Navigation table — a multi-step wizard or flow shows the user's current step and the total or expected number of remaining steps, or an explicit signal that the total is not yet knowable, not only a history trail of completed steps. Distinct from `N16R` (breadcrumbs/sitemap, which addresses the past/where-you-came-from dimension) and `C03` (consistent Next/Continue/Done labeling, which does not require exposing remaining-step count): `N23R` is specifically the forward-looking dimension neither existing rule requires.
- `product`: add `P11` to `references/core.md`'s Purpose & task table — instructional or coaching content for a feature is delivered in-context at the moment a user attempts or needs that specific feature (a short contextual tip, an inline demo), rather than front-loaded as a mandatory upfront tour, walkthrough, or manual read before any real task need exists. Distinct from `P07` (which establishes that first-use, repeat-use, and expert-use needs differ, without specifying *when* instructional content should be delivered) — `P11` is specifically about delivery timing.
- `usability`: add `E09` to `references/core.md`'s Errors & recovery table — a long, sequential, high-consequence procedure prone to memory failure under stress or interruption (safety-critical setup, an infrastructure migration wizard, a multi-field compliance submission) provides an explicit, interactive step-by-step checklist with persistent checked/unchecked state, and surfaces any skipped step for later completion, rather than relying on the user's memory of an implicit sequence. Distinct from `CR01` (a single, immediately-actionable control for an acute emergency) and `PC06` (general interruption/state-resilience for any flow): `E09` addresses the specific failure mode of a *long ordered sequence* under workload, where the risk is a silently skipped step, not a lost place or an un-actionable emergency screen.
- `psychology`: enrich `PC08`'s citation with Norman's perceived-simplicity-vs-operational-simplicity framing and the scientific-calculator-vs-sparse-menu example, sharpening (not replacing) its existing "frequently needed hidden options can increase cognitive and interaction cost" rule — no new rule ID, no behavior change.
- `psychology`: enrich `PC06`'s citation with Norman's "welcome back" contextual-resumption framing and the interrupted-multi-step-form example, sharpening its existing state-preservation-on-interruption rule — no new rule ID, no behavior change.
- `usability`: enrich `AF02`'s citation with the explicit "Desire Lines" naming and Norman's example of users overloading a free-text/Notes field because the structured fields nearby are too rigid — no new rule ID, no behavior change.
- `psychology`: enrich `PB01`/`PB02`'s citations with Norman's opt-in-vs-opt-out pension/401(k)-enrollment example as further named-source backing for the existing defaults rules — no new rule ID, no behavior change.
- `product`: enrich `IA01`/`IA04`'s citations with Norman's expert-centric-taxonomy example (a field guide requiring the user to already know a bird is a "grebe"; medical lab results organized by which lab ran the test rather than by clinical symptom) — no new rule ID, no behavior change.
- `usability`/`product`: enrich `usability/references/core.md`'s `E01` and `product/references/core.md`'s `C04` citations with Norman's "the natural world has no error messages" framing — no new rule ID, no behavior change.
- `usability`: enrich `F10`/`F11`'s citations with Norman's security-usability inverse paradox and the 5,000-password encrypted-file anecdote — no new rule ID, no behavior change.
- `README.md`: add Donald A. Norman's *Living with Complexity* to the "Evidence base" list of comparison-pass sources.
- `CHANGELOG.md`: add an entry in the same style as prior comparison-pass entries, naming what was added, what was confirmed already-covered (affordances/signifiers vocabulary, defaults, error-prevention-over-messages, expert-centric IA mismatch, interruption resilience, desire lines, security-usability tension), and what was deliberately left out and why.
- `package.json` version bump (patch) plus `scripts/sync-version.mjs` and `scripts/build.mjs`/`scripts/validate.mjs` runs, per this project's standing release gate.

Explicitly out of scope, consistent with every prior comparison pass's treatment of process/methodology content: Frontstage/Backstage service architecture, Expressive Service Blueprinting, systems-level service root-cause redesign (Amtrak Acela, iPod ecosystem case studies), physical queue-topology engineering (single-line multi-server routing, spatial/temporal double buffering), distribution-channel/middleman sales bias, and organizational over-measurement critique. These describe how a service organization runs its own operations or sells through a channel, not what a static UI/feature review checks from evidence (a screenshot, description, code, or running build).

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `ux-crux/usability`: gains three new requirements — design-side sign/warning-reliance-as-failure-signal (`AF05`), wait/queue psychological-fairness-and-occupancy (`S13`), forward-step visibility in multi-step flows (`N23R`), and interactive checklists for long high-consequence procedures (`E09`) — plus citation-only enrichments to `AF02`, `E01`, `F10`, and `F11` (no requirement-text behavior change).
- `ux-crux/psychology`: gains one new requirement — cumulative multiplicity/scaling burden of many small interactions (`PC09`) — plus citation-only enrichments to `PC06`, `PC08`, `PB01`, and `PB02` (no requirement-text behavior change).
- `ux-crux/product`: gains two new requirements — activity-centered control grouping for cross-module goals (`IA10`) and just-in-time contextual instructional delivery (`P11`) — plus citation-only enrichments to `IA01`, `IA04`, and `C04` (no requirement-text behavior change).
