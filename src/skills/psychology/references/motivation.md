# Psychology: motivation, commitment, progress

See `../../../shared/evidence-model.md` and `severity-model.md` for how to report findings from these rules. Read `ethics.md` before treating any mechanism below as a recommendation rather than a description.

| ID | Rule | Evidence | Default severity | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PM01 | Before adding motivational pressure, reduce friction and ensure the desired action is feasible; ability can be a stronger lever than more prompts. | Psychological mechanism | Moderate | Stanford Behavior Design Lab, Fogg Behavior Model; NN/g Less Effort, More Completion (EAS Framework) |
| PM02 | Support autonomy: meaningful choices should remain voluntary, understandable, and reversible where possible. | Psychological mechanism + ethical | Major | Ryan & Deci, Self-Determination Theory (2000); NN/g Autonomy, Relatedness, and Competence in UX Design; NN/g Deceptive Patterns in UX |
| PM03 | Support competence with truthful, interpretable progress and feedback that helps users understand what they achieved and what remains. | Psychological mechanism | Moderate | Ryan & Deci, Self-Determination Theory (2000); NN/g Autonomy, Relatedness, and Competence in UX Design |
| PM04 | Use relatedness/social connection only where it serves the user's context; do not force social exposure or comparison into intrinsically private tasks. | Contextual | Moderate | Ryan & Deci, Self-Determination Theory (2000); NN/g Autonomy, Relatedness, and Competence in UX Design |
| PM05 | Prompts should arrive when the user has sufficient context and ability to act; repeated prompts cannot compensate for a difficult or low-value action. | Psychological mechanism | Moderate | Stanford Behavior Design Lab, Fogg Behavior Model; NN/g Interaction Cost: Definition |
| PM06 | Small commitments can support user-owned goals, but commitment devices must remain low-stakes and should not trap users into escalation. | Contextual + ethical | Major | NN/g The Principle of Commitment and Behavioral Consistency; NN/g Deceptive Patterns in UX |
| PM07 | Progress indicators should represent real progress toward a user-recognizable goal; do not fabricate completion pressure. | Strong + ethical | Major | NN/g The Principle of Commitment and Behavioral Consistency; NN/g Deceptive Patterns in UX |
| PM08 | When a flow asks a user to commit to a real-world or offline follow-through action, provide a concrete execution-intention prompt — a specific time, place, or scheduling field ("when will you do this?") — rather than relying on persuasive copy or a risk warning alone. | Strong | Moderate | Kurt Lewin, channel factors; Leventhal, Singer & Jones, Yale tetanus-inoculation study (1965) |
| PM09 | A head-start progress framing (crediting steps the user has already genuinely completed or provided, so a progress indicator starts above zero) is a legitimate, distinct mechanism from PM07's fabricated-progress violation, as long as the true total effort required is unchanged and the framing accurately reflects real completed or credited steps. A head-start that changes the true total effort required, or that credits steps not actually completed, is still a PM07 violation. | Strong | Moderate | Nunes & Drèze, "The Endowed Progress Effect: How Artificial Advancement Increases Effort" (2006) |

## How the psychology skill should apply these

Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness — PM02-PM04) is a strong, well-replicated foundation; translate it cautiously and concretely into the reviewed product rather than as a slogan. PM01/PM05 encode the Fogg model's insight that ability often beats added motivation — before recommending "add more encouragement," check whether the actual blocker is that the action is hard, not that the user is unmotivated. When a target behavior is not occurring at all, apply Fogg's own troubleshooting order: check whether a Trigger is present and reaches the user first, then whether Ability is the blocker (the action is too hard), and only last consider whether Motivation itself needs boosting — boosting motivation before ruling out a missing trigger or a hard action wastes effort and can read as nagging. PM08 sharpens this further for real-world/offline follow-through specifically: even when trigger, ability, and motivation all look sufficient, a flow that stops at persuasive copy without a concrete "when/where" prompt leaves the intention-action gap unbridged. PM06 and PM07 are the ones most prone to becoming exploitative if applied uncritically (fake progress, forced escalating commitment): run both through the four-gate test in `ethics.md` before endorsing them, and treat a fabricated-progress finding as an ethical-guardrail violation, not a style note. PM09 draws the line PM07 needs but does not itself state: a truthful head start is not the same failure as fabricated progress, and should not be flagged as one — check specifically whether the total required effort changed and whether the credited steps were real before deciding which of the two rules applies.
