# Psychology: attention and Gestalt perception

See `../../shared/evidence-model.md` and `severity-model.md` for how to report findings from these rules. Read `ethics.md` before treating any mechanism below as a recommendation rather than a description.

| ID | Rule | Evidence | Default severity | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PA01 | Visual hierarchy should make task priority — not business pressure — the most salient path. | Strong + ethical | Major | NN/g 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX; NN/g The Vortex |
| PA02 | Important feedback/state changes should occur near the user's current locus of attention or use a proportionate cue; do not assume distant changes will be noticed. | Strong | Major | NN/g Change Blindness in UX |
| PA03 | Avoid multiple simultaneous salient changes, animations, badges, or competing CTAs that dilute attention. | Strong | Moderate | NN/g Change Blindness in UX; NN/g Minimize Cognitive Load |
| PA04 | Use motion, contrast, scale, and isolation deliberately; if everything is emphasized, priority becomes ambiguous. | Strong | Moderate | NN/g 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX; NN/g Change Blindness in UX |
| PA05 | Use proximity to express semantic grouping; labels/actions should sit close to the objects they describe or affect. | Strong | Major | NN/g Closeness of Actions and Objects in GUI Design; NN/g Form Design Quick Fix: Group Form Elements Using White Space |
| PA06 | Use similarity consistently: visually similar controls should behave similarly; do not style unrelated/noninteractive objects like actions. | Strong | Major | NN/g Similarity Principle in Visual Design; NN/g 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX |
| PA07 | Use common regions/containers only when they represent real conceptual groups; strong boundaries can override proximity cues. | Strong | Moderate | NN/g The Principle of Common Region |
| PA08 | Ensure figure/ground and contrast make primary content and interactive affordances distinguishable in all supported appearances. | Strong | Major | NN/g Visual Design in UX: Study Guide; Apple HIG Color |
| PA09 | Do not style legitimate, user-relevant content (announcements, featured items, in-app messages) using standard ad-banner conventions — a fixed horizontal strip at a typical ad aspect ratio, a flashy standalone box, or an ad-like "Sponsored"/badge treatment. Selective attention trained on real ads causes users to filter this content out entirely ("banner blindness"), the opposite failure from PA03/PA04's over-emphasis concern. | Strong | Major | NN/g Banner Blindness: Old and New Findings; NN/g 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX |
| PA10 | A single, uniform column of dense text naturally produces an F-shaped scanning bias: users concentrate fixations on the top and left, and systematically neglect right-side and lower content. Breaking dense text into structured multi-column blocks or distinct visual sections disperses fixations across the full width and depth of the page instead of leaving it to a narrow top-left band. | Strong | Major | Romano Bergstrom & Schall (eds.), *Eye Tracking in User Experience Design* (2014) |
| PA11 | An ad or third-party/sponsored element is not styled with the same shape, color, and typography as the product's own primary CTA controls; per the Law of Similarity, matching visual language causes users to mistake the ad for a genuine control and click it unintentionally. Distinct from PA06 (which addresses noninteractive objects wrongly styled as actions in general) and the mirror-image of PA09 (legitimate content styled like an ad and therefore ignored) — here, an ad is styled like a genuine, trusted control and therefore mistakenly clicked. | Strong + ethical | Major | Gestalt Law of Similarity; Falbe, Frederiksen & Andersen, *White Hat UX* (Disguised Ads pattern) |
| PA12 | Do not rely on an auto-rotating image/content carousel to communicate important information or a primary call-to-action; users systematically fail to notice or interact with panels beyond the first one shown. | Strong | Moderate | Falbe, Frederiksen & Andersen, *White Hat UX*; NN/g Carousels/Auto-Forwarding content research |
| PA13 | A dynamically inserted, unrelated element (an advertisement, an auto-generated recommendation, or a promotional module) is not placed directly adjacent to sensitive, serious, or emotionally weighted user content when the juxtaposition itself would plausibly create an unintended narrative or emotional meaning neither element carries on its own. Distinct from PA09/PA11: those concern an element being visually mistaken for something else (ignored as an ad, or mistaken for a genuine control); PA13 concerns unintended meaning created by placing two correctly-identified, unrelated elements next to each other. | Strong + ethical | Major | Lev Kuleshov, Kuleshov effect (film-editing juxtaposition research) |
| PA14 | Elements arranged along a continuous line, curve, or implied path are perceived as one connected sequence (Gestalt Continuation); a control or a piece of related content that visually interrupts an otherwise continuous path — without a stated reason — reads as detached from the sequence even when it is functionally part of it. Distinct from PA05 (Proximity, spatial closeness) and PA07 (Common Region, shared boundary): Continuation is about a perceived line or flow, not distance or containment. | Strong | Moderate | Max Wertheimer; Kurt Koffka; Wolfgang Köhler, Gestalt Continuation |
| PA15 | Users mentally complete a familiar but visually incomplete shape, icon, or pattern (Gestalt Closure); an intentionally partial graphic used to stand in for a full concept must remain unambiguous once closure is applied — a fragment plausible as more than one completed shape is a Closure failure, not a stylistic choice. | Strong | Moderate | Max Wertheimer; Kurt Koffka; Wolfgang Köhler, Gestalt Closure |
| PA16 | Elements that move, animate, or point in the same direction at the same time are perceived as belonging to one group or process (Gestalt Common Fate); do not let unrelated elements share synchronized motion or directional cues that imply a false relationship, and do not let functionally related elements fail to share a common-fate cue where doing so would clarify that they belong together. Distinct from PA06 (Similarity, static visual likeness): Common Fate groups by shared movement, not shared appearance. | Strong | Moderate | Max Wertheimer; Kurt Koffka; Wolfgang Köhler, Gestalt Common Fate |
| PA17 | Users automatically simplify a complex layout into a small number of balanced, symmetrical regions (Gestalt Symmetry & Order) before consciously parsing its content; a layout whose visual symmetry groups functionally unrelated controls together fights this automatic simplification and should be flagged, distinct from PA05/PA07 (which concern spatial closeness and shared containers, not the eye's automatic search for balance). | Strong | Moderate | Max Wertheimer; Kurt Koffka; Wolfgang Köhler, Gestalt Symmetry and Order; Mark Wells, *User Experience Design* |
| PA18 | A chart or visual display used for precise quantitative comparison encodes values by position along a common scale or by length, not by angle, area, volume, or colour intensity, because people judge the former most accurately. Distinct from the grouping rules PA05-PA17: PA18 concerns how accurately a value is read, not how elements group. Does not apply where a rough proportion is enough; a chart the evidence does not show is `NOT ASSESSABLE`. | Strong | Moderate | Cleveland & McGill, "Graphical Perception: Theory, Experimentation, and Application to the Development of Graphical Methods", *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 79(387) (1984), 531-554 |
| PA19 | A bar chart, in which length encodes value, has a value axis that starts at zero; a truncated axis makes viewers perceive differences as larger, and the effect persists after viewers are warned. Distinct from PA18, which concerns which attribute encodes a value; PA19 concerns the scale baseline. Does not apply to line charts or to a clearly marked axis break the viewer cannot misread; an axis the evidence does not show is `NOT ASSESSABLE`. | Strong | Moderate | Yang, Vargas Restrepo, Stanley & Marsh, "Truncating Bar Graphs Persistently Misleads Viewers", *Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition* 10(2) (2021), 298-311; via Kole Jain, "7 UI/UX mistakes that SCREAM you're a beginner" |
| PA20 | A homepage carousel does not auto-rotate on mobile (it is advanced by swipe and visible controls); on desktop, auto-rotation pauses on mouse hover and stops once the user has changed a slide. Distinct from PA12, which concerns relying on a carousel to carry important content. Does not apply to static homepage banners and content sections; keyboard-focus pausing, which the evidence does not show is `NOT ASSESSABLE`. | Strong | Moderate | Baymard Institute, Scott, "10 UX Requirements to Follow for a User-Friendly Homepage Carousel Design" (2019, updated 2025) |
| PA21 | Content the user needs for the task does not share a region, rail or visual section with advertisements or promotions, because users skip a whole region once one item in it reads as an ad; important content is not placed next to ads or in the typical ad positions without a check that users still see it. Distinct from PA09, which concerns styling real content like an ad banner, and PA13, which concerns the meaning created by placing an ad beside sensitive content, not users skipping needed content that sits beside real ads. Does not apply to a page with no ads or promotions; how a particular audience scans the region the evidence does not show is `NOT ASSESSABLE`. | Strong | Moderate | Nielsen Norman Group, Pernice, "Banner Blindness Revisited: Users Dodge Ads on Mobile and Desktop" (2018) |

## How the psychology skill should apply these

These are Gestalt/attention mechanics: what a user's visual system groups, notices, and ignores automatically, before any conscious reasoning happens. Most of these (PA02-PA08) are corrective — get the grouping and salience right so the interface communicates truthfully — and need only a light pass through the four-gate test in `ethics.md`. PA01 is the one to watch most carefully: when "make it salient" is being used to override the user's own task priority in favor of a business metric (e.g. an upsell CTA styled more prominently than the task the user came to do), treat it as failing the ethics gate's user-benefit/agency test, not just a visual-design nitpick. PA09 is the mirror-image failure to PA01/PA03/PA04: those are about a business making one thing *too* salient; PA09 is about important content becoming *invisible* because its styling accidentally matches the pattern users have trained themselves to ignore. Check PA09 whenever the reviewed evidence contains an in-app announcement, promotional callout, or featured-content module styled as a boxed banner. PA10 is a distinct, structural failure mode from PA01/PA04: those are about deliberate salience choices, while PA10 is about a default scanning bias that shows up even when nothing on the page is trying to be salient at all. Check PA10 whenever the evidence is a dense, single-column, text-heavy page (an article, a long informational page) where important content or links sit on the right side or far down the page — that placement alone can make otherwise well-designed content invisible to normal scanning behavior. Check PA11 whenever the evidence contains third-party or sponsored content near the product's own primary CTAs — since a disguised ad is a deception mechanism (the ad is interactive, but deceptively styled to be mistaken for a trusted control), always run it through `ethics.md`'s four-gate test before treating a fix as anything but an ethical-guardrail finding, the same way PA09 already is. Check PA12 whenever the evidence shows an auto-rotating carousel carrying a primary message or CTA in any panel after the first. Check PA13 whenever the evidence shows dynamically inserted or automatically placed content (an ad, a recommendation module, an auto-generated "related" item) sitting immediately next to sensitive, tragic, or serious user content — the two elements can each be exactly what they claim to be (a real ad is a real ad, real content is real content) and the finding still holds, because the failure is the meaning created by their juxtaposition, not either element's own identity. This is what distinguishes PA13 from PA09 (content wrongly ignored because it looks like an ad) and PA11 (an ad wrongly trusted because it looks like a control): PA13 requires no misidentification at all. PA14-PA17 complete this project's coverage of the seven classic Gestalt perception laws, alongside the already-covered Proximity (PA05), Similarity (PA06), and Common Region (PA07) — Figure/Ground (PA08) is the eighth commonly cited law this file also already covers. Check PA14 (Continuation) whenever the evidence shows a sequence of elements arranged along a line, curve, or path (a stepper, a timeline, a row of related cards) where one item breaks the visual flow without explanation. Check PA15 (Closure) whenever the evidence uses a deliberately incomplete icon, logo, or graphic to represent a concept — confirm the intended completed shape is the only plausible reading, not one of several. Check PA16 (Common Fate) whenever multiple elements animate or move together: flag synchronized motion between functionally unrelated elements (a false-grouping risk) and flag related elements that would benefit from shared motion but currently animate independently. Check PA17 (Symmetry & Order) whenever the evidence shows a deliberately balanced or symmetrical layout: confirm the regions the eye naturally simplifies the layout into actually correspond to real functional groupings, not an accidental grouping of unrelated controls. PA18 is an encoding-accuracy check: ask what the viewer must compare precisely and whether the chart encodes it with position or length.

Check PA19 whenever a bar chart appears: read the first axis value before judging the visual difference between bars.

Baymard Institute research rules added in this file: PA20. Check each when the evidence shows its surface (forms and checkout, navigation, search and filters, accounts and orders, delivery and returns); each reports `NOT ASSESSABLE` for parts the evidence does not show.

Nielsen Norman Group research rules added in this file: PA21. Check each when the evidence shows its surface; each reports `NOT ASSESSABLE` for parts the evidence does not show.
